What the research settled

Three things hold across all four niches, and they are the reason a single layout was never going to feel tailored.

Home is about the store, not the program. Starbucks, Chipotle, Dutch Bros, Dermis, Round1 — the home screen leads with what's new, what's on, or what you're owed today. Earned rewards live on their own tab. Atlas currently leads Home with rewards, spin and streak, which is the app talking about itself. Every preset below moves offers or the membership card to the top and keeps TopRewardsGrid to a short "redeem now" strip.

Purchase frequency decides the mechanic. Dispensary regulars visit about every 8 days; medspa patients about three times a year at $350–550 a visit; a theater treats eight visits a year as "frequent." Streaks only make sense in the first column. Chipotle is the sole national chain running true streaks, and only as a summer campaign. Not one of the seven medspa programs or eight entertainment programs uses them.

Phone number is the universal fallback at the register. Dutch Bros, Square, Toast, Bowlero, Topgolf, Alpine IQ, Dutchie and Evolus all identify a member by phone number when there is no scan. Atlas already enforces one account per phone (CP-59 normalize_phone), so a phone-number lookup on the desk is a small addition, and it should replace the backup code as the thing staff are taught first.

Preset 1 · Smoke shop

The closest proven analogs are dispensary platforms (springbig, Alpine IQ, Dutchie) and the regional tobacco chains (Wild Bill's, Smoker Friendly, Smokers Choice). Their programs are deal feeds with a punch card underneath: Wild Bill's rewards page is a list of "buy 5, get the 6th" coupons; Smoker Friendly runs category punch clubs (papers buy 5 → $5 off, vape buy 4 → $5 off) plus a monthly drawing; Trulieve pays a bonus for shopping five times a month. Visit count, not spend, is the lever.

Tabs
Home · Deals · Check in · Rewards · Streak. Deals is LimitedOffersSection promoted to a full tab (billboard layout), sorted by expiry.
Home, top to bottom

1. LiveMemberCard with QR, points, and "N visits to next reward".

2. Today's deal — one FeaturedOfferBanner-style hero that rotates daily. Empty state shows this week's deal instead; never blank.

3. Punch clubs — new compact module: per-category visit counters (3 of 5). This is the visit-based reward Smoker Friendly and Wild Bill's use; implement as reward rules with rule_type = 'visit' filtered by tag, or reuse streak milestones scoped to a category.

4. "Ready to claim" strip — TopRewardsGrid limited to affordable rewards.

5. StreakMini as a single compact row, then LocationCard.

Earning defaults
1 pt per $1; first reward at ~150 pts (a free lighter, papers, or $5 off glass) so the first redemption happens on visit 3–4; birthday 100 pts; referral 200 pts. Weekly streak (not daily) with the 4-week reward road from the demo pack.

Deals tabPunch clubsWeekly streakWin-back textsBirthdayReferralSpin (keep as "monthly drawing")Membership tabRaffle on HomeSpend tiersMerch catalog
Watch-outs
Rewards must be discounts on accessories, glass, CBD or store credit — FDA bars non-tobacco giveaways tied to cigarette purchases. Age-gate the app (21+) and keep any SMS behind a birthdate opt-in; App Store rule 1.4.3 forbids apps that encourage tobacco consumption, so copy stays about the store and its deals, never the product's use. Google Play bans apps that facilitate tobacco sales — no cart.

Preset 2 · Food shop

Coffee, boba, pizza, bakery, smoothie. The formula is remarkably consistent across Starbucks, Dutch Bros, Chick-fil-A, Chipotle and Sweetgreen: a balance strip on top, one activatable offer or challenge, then what's new. Navigation is Home · Scan · Order · Rewards/Offers · More. The lessons that matter for a small shop are Sweetgreen killing its $10/month tier because complexity killed enrollment, Chipotle's 85-point tortilla as a deliberately cheap first rung, and Dutch Bros accepting a phone number at the window when you forget to scan.

Tabs
Home · Offers · Check in · Rewards · Streak. Offers = LimitedOffersSection as a tab (coupon layout) plus NewsSection underneath it — "what's new" and "deals" belong together.
Home, top to bottom

1. LiveMemberCard with QR, points, "1 order from a free slice".

2. New this week hero — a product promotion, not a reward. Uses the featured-offer slot; the builder labels it "Promote a product".

3. Active offer, tap to start — Dutch Bros / Starbucks pattern: one challenge card ("order 3× this month → free drink"). Maps to an automated offer with a visit goal.

4. "Redeem now" strip (TopRewardsGrid, affordable only).

5. StreakMini + DailySpinButton as the existing side-by-side pair, moved below the fold. 6. LocationCard.

Earning defaults
10 pts per $1 (the QSR convention — bigger numbers feel better on small tickets); first reward at 200–300 pts (a free side or drink, ≈ two or three visits); sign-up gift on first check-in; birthday reward; 180-day inactivity expiry with a reminder push at 14 days out.

Chipotle sees only ~20% of in-restaurant orders scanned versus ~90% of digital — the counter is where loyalty leaks. Phone lookup and a two-second desk flow matter more here than any home-screen decision.

Preset 3 · Medspa & aesthetics

This is Dermis territory, and what makes Dermis feel tailored is that membership is a treatment allowance, not points: the patient sees "You have 2 treatments available", redeems by QR at the front desk, and gets pushed when the allowance is about to reset. RepeatMD does the same with banked monthly credit ($99 / $199 / $249 tiers). The brand programs (Allē, ASPIRE, Evolus) add the other retention lever: a treatment-cycle clock — Evolus literally issues a $40 reward every 90 days. None of them has streaks, spin, daily check-ins or leaderboards.

Tabs
Home · Book · Member · Rewards · Profile. Re-enable the existing BOOK tab (GHL booking link) and promote MembershipSection to its own tab. No Streaks tab, no Check-in tab — check-in QR lives on the member card.
Home, top to bottom

1. Membership card — tier name, treatments or credit available this cycle, renewal date. Non-members see the join card with "less than one visit a month" math.

2. Next treatment due — new module keyed to last treatment date + cycle (tox 12 wks, filler 6–12 mo, facial 4 wks) with one Book button. Data: a treatment category on the award (desk picks "Tox / Filler / Facial / Laser / Other"), cycle length per category in the builder.

3. Wallet strip: points, credit, check-in QR (LiveMemberCard compact).

4. One offer with expiry countdown (FeaturedOfferBanner — Dermis expires the first reward in a day on purpose).

5. ReferralProgressCard + review nudge. 6. Packages & memberships (MembershipSection summary).

Earning defaults
1 pt per $1; sign-up 50; check-in 80; Google review 60; referral 200 (Dermis's published defaults). First reward at 50 pts = $25 off, second at ~200 = a free add-on treatment. No points on membership purchases (Vagaro precedent). Membership: monthly only, 3-month minimum, one included treatment plus 10–20% off, per-unit tox discount.

Watch-outs
Only 55–57% of first-time tox patients return for a second visit; rebooking at checkout runs 40–69%. The "next treatment due" card and its push are the single feature that changes revenue here. Membership cancellation friction is RepeatMD's biggest complaint — make cancelling easy and visible.

Preset 4 · Entertainment

Bowling, arcades, trampoline parks, escape rooms, mini golf, batting cages. The operators that actually monetize retention sell a recurring pass priced at about one visit — Urban Air $14.99/mo, Chuck E. Cheese $7.99–29.99/mo, Topgolf PlayMore $20/mo, AMC A-List — with family sharing, bring-a-friend and food discounts. Points-only programs (Bowlero MVB, Round1) are thin. Bowlero's real retention tool is a fixed weekly specials calendar: Family Unlimited weekends, unlimited nights Mon–Thu, Friday After Party. Everything earns by the visit, not the dollar: Regal's first status is at visit 6, D&B caps food points per visit.

Tabs
Home · Events · Check in · Pass · Rewards. Events = specials calendar + upcoming events + "Book a party" (NewsSection reworked as a dated list). Pass = MembershipSection with family members and bring-a-friend.
Home, top to bottom

1. Pass card — QR, pass name (or "no pass"), visits toward next perk.

2. This week — day-of-week specials strip (new module; the builder gets a 7-day grid, each day an optional deal).

3. Coming up — next two events; league nights, glow bowl, tournaments.

4. Book a party CTA — links to their booking page or GHL form (reuse BOOK logic).

5. Pass upsell one-liner with the CenterEdge math: price the pass near one visit so it pays off in under two.

6. Bring-a-friend referral (ReferralProgressCard, referral reward = 50% off friend's visit).

Earning defaults
Visit stamps, not dollar points: 5th visit = free game / hour. Birthday reward. Pass: good–better–best monthly (e.g. $9 / $19 / $29 — solo, family, unlimited+food), month-to-month after a 3-month minimum, shareable across household cards, member-only events.
Keep / drop
Pass tabWeekly specials gridEvents listBook a partyVisit stampsBring a friendBirthdayRaffle (tournament prize)Spin (kids love it — only if the owner asks)StreaksDollar points with expiryNine-level tiers / leaderboards
Watch-outs
Groups drive the visit: Topgolf's pass covers six guests, Urban Air adds five party guests. Anything that only rewards the account holder undersells the occasion. Apple/Google Wallet passes drive 60% of reloads before arrival in Embed's data — a wallet pass is a better long-term identity token here than the app alone.

How this lands in Atlas

None of this is four apps. It is one businesses.layout_preset column (smoke · food · medspa · entertainment · custom) that drives three things: which tabs tabsForConfig() returns, the order and visibility of Home modules on app/[business]/app/page.tsx, and which builder sections are shown. The demo packs in lib/demo-packs.ts already know the niche, so instant demos pick the preset automatically.

  1. Desk guardrails + phone lookup. Confirm step on removals over 500 pts, a manager-only "reset program" action with a real confirm, and phone-number lookup as the second search on the desk (the code already normalizes phones). Small, and it's the direct answer to what happened this week.
  2. Layout presets. The column, the tab sets, the Home module order, and a "Layout" picker at the top of the builder. Food and smoke presets are mostly reordering; medspa and entertainment need the two new modules below.
  3. Two new modules. Weekly specials grid (entertainment; also useful for food) and treatment-due reminder (medspa; needs a category on the award and a cycle length per category). Punch clubs for smoke shops can start as tagged visit rewards.
  4. Builder cleanup. The Design tab has grown to 17 knobs (card_style, button_style, banner_style, background_pattern, pattern_color, badge_style, heading_style, divider_style, offer_card_style, reward_card_style, points_card_style, three layouts, three streak themes). Fold them into three groups — Theme (preset + colors + pattern), Cards (card, button, offer, reward, points-card styles), Sections (layouts, headings, dividers, badges) — with the ten one-click theme presets on top and everything else behind "Advanced". Presets should set all of these at once so a rep never opens Advanced at a door.
  5. Then sell. The pitch per niche writes itself from the table above, and the demo a rep builds at the door already looks like the niche's dream app instead of a generic one.

On Clover, plainly. There are two different things on that "Select a platform" screen. Web means an app that lives in the merchant's Clover web dashboard and talks to Clover's API — it does not put anything on the register screen. The device boxes (Station, Mini, Flex) mean an Android app that runs on the Clover terminal itself. That second one is what solves the Khaled problem: your front-desk page in a thin Clover Android wrapper, one tap from the register's home screen, with the same USB scanner plugged into the Station. No second laptop, no points automation, no POS data at all — just your desk where the staff already are. It's a listing-and-review process (weeks) and a small Capacitor-style build you've done twice already, so it belongs after the presets, not before.
